## Rules reconciliation (the big ones)

4. ⚑ **Zone geometry is a 3-cell line: Your Home ↔ Neutral ↔ Enemy Home.** "Adjacent" means one step along that line. Each player sees the same board mirrored. — v1.2 names three zones and cards reference "adjacent zones"; a line is the simplest geometry that makes both true.
5. ⚑ **Movement exists (v1.2 defines none — the game cannot function without it): as a Main-phase action, the active player moves one of their ready units to an adjacent zone; the move exhausts the unit.** — Without movement, units can never reach each other and no combat ever occurs. Exhaust-on-move makes positioning a real cost (march *or* fight, not both) and keeps Rush valuable.
6. ⚑ **"Base" = the player.** Damage to your base reduces your Life (start 20); "heal your base" restores Life up to the starting value. A unit may attack the enemy base **only from inside that enemy's Home zone**. Guard units in that zone must be attacked first, per the Guard rule. — Half the card pool references "base"; mapping it to Life is the smallest bridge. Requiring Home-zone presence makes the zones and the march matter.
7. ⚑ **Summoning sickness: a unit cannot attack or move during the turn it entered play. Rush lifts both restrictions.** — Implied by Rush's v1.2 definition ("can attack the turn it is played"); extending Rush to movement keeps Red's tempo identity.
8. ⚑ **The Main Phase and Combat Phase are merged.** Main Phase = strict action alternation, active player first; consecutive passes end the phase. **Attack is one action**, available only to the active player, resolved immediately using v1.2's combat-phase procedure. — v1.2 literally lists "an attack is one action" inside the Main Phase, which contradicts a separate sequential Combat Phase; alternation matches the designer's LotR-TCG inspiration and "interaction every round" goal. Cards granting "an extra combat phase" instead ready your units mid-turn.
9. ⚑ **Off-turn plays are allowed:** during the alternation the non-active player may play cards and activate abilities (not attack, move, or resource). — This is what makes the alternation meaningful interaction rather than watching; consistent with "each player alternates taking an action."
10. ⚑ **An attack is one attacker → one target.** Damage is simultaneous both ways; Armor reduces incoming damage; attacking the base draws no counter-damage. Multi-unit attacks are deferred. — "An attack is one action"; v1.2 never says how a multi-unit attack assigns the defender's counter-damage. One-on-one keeps defenders meaningful and the engine unambiguous.
11. ⚑ **Guard: while the defending player has a ready (non-imprisoned) Guard unit in the contested zone, attacks in that zone must target a Guard unit; the base may not be attacked past a Guard.** — Direct v1.2 reading, with "imprisoned units cannot defend" applied.
12. ⚑ **Breakthrough N: when the attacker destroys the defending unit, excess damage — up to N — is dealt to the defender's controller's Life.** Works from any zone. — v1.2 defines the mechanic without a number while every Red card carries one; "cap at N" is the reading that gives N meaning.
13. ⚑ **Overextend N on units: +N Power while attacking if it is the only friendly unit in its zone.** — v1.2 says "gains bonus when attacking alone"; under one-attacker-per-action *every* attack is "alone," so the surviving meaningful reading is positional isolation (the unit literally over-extended past its lines).
14. ⚑ **Overextend N on actions/upgrades: playing the card shifts Influence N toward your opponent.** — Undefined in v1.2 yet printed on nearly every Red action; the spreadsheet's Red identity is "burning Influence" for power. This makes Red fast but self-destructive — exactly the stated volatility goal. Balance flag: Red's whole suite feeds the opponent's Influence win; watch it in play.
15. ⚑ **Influence is one shared signed track (+15 you / −15 opponent).** A unit/upgrade printed "Influence: +N" grants its controller N Influence **once, when it enters play**. "Opponent loses N Influence" = you gain N. — The spreadsheet note "make sure to denote when they get the influence" confirms timing was unsettled; on-play is the simplest deterministic answer.
16. ⚑ **Prison:** imprisoned units can't attack, defend, move, or use abilities; they deal no counter-damage and their Guard stops protecting. Release when: the imprisoning *unit* leaves play (unit-sourced prisons), an effect releases them, or **the jailer's Influence drops below 0** (default release threshold, since no card specifies one). Prison decay per v1.2: at the start of the jailer's turn, −1 Influence per unit they hold. Cards that restate the decay ("At the start of your turn, lose 1 Influence") are treated as reminder text, not a second charge. — Action-sourced prisons can't use "source leaves play" (the action is discarded instantly), so they persist until decay/negative influence breaks them.
17. ⚑ **Win-condition checks run after every atomic state change. Order: Life first, then Influence.** If both players would hit 0 Life simultaneously, the player who took the action wins. — CLAUDE.md's own example question; "acting player wins ties" is deterministic and rewards the aggressor.
18. ⚑ **Resources:** any card may be resourced face-up, 1 per turn in your Resource Phase (plus 2 during setup); every resource is worth 1 while colored costs don't exist in the rules; pay a cost of N by exhausting N ready resources; resources ready in the Reset Phase. Face-down resourcing is omitted until colored costs exist. — The face-up/face-down distinction only does work once resource *symbols* have rules meaning.
19. ⚑ **First-player compensation: the first player draws 1 (not 2) on their first Draw Phase** (`firstTurnDraw` parameter). — Acting first in an alternation game plus a full draw is a big edge; this is the cheapest standard mitigation and it's tunable.
20. ⚑ **Empty deck: drawing from an empty deck simply draws nothing — no loss.** — Forgiving default for playtests; flagged as a designer question (mill-as-loss changes deck design).
21. ⚑ **No hand limit, no mulligan** (v1.2 has neither). **Unit damage persists** between turns; "heal X" removes marked damage; base healing caps at starting Life.
22. ⚑ **Ranged: may attack units in its own zone or an adjacent zone; never bases; attacking exhausts as normal.** — The literal "adjacent zones only" would forbid same-zone attacks, which makes cheap ranged units incoherent; no Blue cards ship tonight so the risk is low. Flagged.
23. ⚑ **Card-text reinterpretations (each flagged in the card browser):** *Radiant Citadel* — "your maximum Influence is increased by 2" has no effect on a ±15 shared track; implemented as *"your opponent's Influence win threshold becomes 17 while this is in play"* (defensive, matches the wall theme). *Flying* (Light's Vanguard, undefined anywhere) — implemented as *"may move to any zone, ignoring adjacency."* *"Can't attack players"* (Binding Light) = can't attack bases. — Minimal-surgery readings so the printed cards function; all need real design.
24. ⚑ **All targets are chosen when an action is submitted; the engine never pauses mid-resolution for input. Strictly-beneficial "may" effects auto-apply.** — Keeps the engine a pure deterministic reducer (same inputs → same game), which is what makes replay, undo, and simulation work.
25. ⚑ **Deck lists:** Yellow = all 48 uniques ×1. Red = 36 uniques + a second copy of each of the twelve cost-1/cost-2 cards = 48. — Meets the 48-card minimum deterministically; Red's curve wants cheap duplicates.

## Turn-structure rework (2026-07-08, builder session #3)

40. ✅ **Round-based turns with claimable initiative** (builder design, SWU-style; supersedes rules-v1.2 turn structure). A *round* = start steps (initiative player first, then opponent: resolve start-of-turn effects, ready all cards, draw 2, optionally bank 1), then strictly alternating single actions — play a card, move, attack, activate, claim initiative, or pass — unlimited per round, resource-constrained. **Claim initiative** spends your action, takes the token, and passes you out for the rest of the round; you go first next round. Passes are soft (opponent acting reopens yours); round ends on consecutive passes. Unclaimed initiative stays with its holder. Old phase ladder (Reset/Draw/Resource/Main/Combat/End) and per-player turns dissolve; "start/end of turn" card text re-templates to "your start step" / "end of round."
44. ✅ **No round-1 draw asymmetry** (builder ruling). Alternating actions + claim-cost dissolve the first-mover advantage that `firstTurnDraw: 1` compensated for; everyone draws 2 in every start step. Parameter renamed `firstRoundDraw`, default = `drawPerTurn`, kept as a one-line nerf lever if initiative proves too strong in playtests.
43. ✅ **"Extra turn" re-templates to "ready one unit + extra action"** (builder ruling; rounds have no extra turns). Final Onslaught (the only `extraTurn` card): *Ready one of your units, then immediately take an extra action.* Bounded haymaker — one unit activates twice; a full-board ready was rejected because unlimited actions/round would multiply it into a second alpha strike. Engine: `extraTurn` op → `ready(one)` + `extraAction`; `pendingExtraTurn` → `pendingExtraAction`.
42. ✅ **Multi-unit attack + intercept, lean v1** (builder + designer intent from decision 38; supersedes rules-v1.2 Combat Phase and Guard's forced targeting). One attack action: (1) *Declare* — any number of ready units in one zone attack one target (enemy unit there, or the base if it's the enemy Home); attackers exhaust; Overextend declared per unit. (2) *Intercept prompt* — defender may redirect the whole attack to one ready unit they control in that zone; intercepting exhausts, except **Guard**, re-templated to "intercepts without exhausting." (3) *Resolve* — combined power hits as one hit (armor applies once to the total — massing beats armor by design); target counter-punches the highest-power attacker (play-order tiebreak), automatic. Single attack = N=1 case. onDefend influence fires on intercepting or being attacked. First mid-action prompt: paired actions `attack-declared` → `intercept-response`. Parameters for iteration: `interceptExhausts` (true), `counterAssignment` (auto), `armorPerAttack` (once), `maxAttackers` (∞).
41. ✅ **Units enter play ready; Rush = exhaust-free move on entry round** (builder ruling, restoring the original note "may move without exhausting the turn it enters play"). Baseline: any unit can act the round it's played by spending a later action (moving/attacking exhausts as usual). Rush waives the *exhaust* on that unit's move — not the action cost — the round it enters, so it can march and still attack later that round. Whether Rush's waiver also covers attack is a parameter (`rushCoversAttack`, default **false** — move only for now). **Clarified 2026-07-09 (v2.1):** the waiver is the unit's *first* move only — one free reposition — **not** a whole-round pass. A second move the same round exhausts it. (As originally coded the waiver keyed on the whole entry round, letting a fresh Rush unit move unboundedly — the "multiple actions" a playtester spotted.) **SUPERSEDED 2026-07-17 (issue #105, Griff's ruling): Rush is now a static — the *first move each round* is free, refreshing every round the unit stays in play, not just the entry round. The first-move-only and "does not cover attack / does not let it attack early" clauses carry over unchanged; only the entry-round gate is lifted.**

31. ✅ **Setup resources are a player choice** (decided by the builder from playtest experience, 2026-07-08, superseding the ⚑ auto-bank simplification in game-rules §1.3): after drawing 7, each player picks which `startingResources` (2) cards to bank, first player choosing first. A `chooseStartingResources` rules parameter (default **true**) preserves the auto-bank mode for A/B testing. — The choice is real strategy (bank your two dead cards vs. keep options) and the zero-input setup was only ever a shortcut.

## Product / engineering

26. ⚑ **Card definitions are engine data (structured effect JSON, not free text).** The DB seeds from the engine's card set; the admin UI edits numbers (cost, stats, keyword values, influence amounts) and cosmetic text; effect *structure* changes stay in code. A game snapshots its cards and rules at creation, so mid-game edits can't corrupt running games. — Exactly the GENESYS tension: free-text effects can't be validated; structured effects keep new cards from silently breaking games.
27. ⚑ **Rules parameters are a versioned DB row** (starting life, thresholds, draw counts, …, as one JSON config). Admin can edit; new games pick up the new values; running games keep their snapshot. — GENESYS asks for "versions of the rules for easy testing."
28. ⚑ **Games are event-sourced**: the action log is the save; state is replayable from (seed + decks + actions). Enables reconnect, undo (either player may rewind one action — trust-based playtest tool, visibly logged), and future replay/analysis.
29. ⚑ **Random-playout simulation ships in the engine test suite**: N seeded games with random legal actions must terminate with a winner and no errors — the GENESYS sim harness in miniature, and the hook where an AI player later plugs in (an AI is a smarter action-chooser over the same `getLegalActions`).
30. ⚑ **Dev DB = local Homebrew Postgres; prod = any `DATABASE_URL` (Neon-ready with `DIRECT_URL` for migrations); tests demand a separate `DATABASE_URL_TEST` and abort if it matches dev.** Docker image + fly.io config provided; docker-compose file included for machines without local Postgres.

45. ✅ *(format superseded by 46 — the "agent validates, no CI" philosophy carries forward)* **Card config stays a flat CSV edited directly on GitHub; no validation CI** (builder, 2026-07-08; refines ⚑26 / 📋39). The designer commits `data/cards.csv` edits in-browser; the builder/agent validates **on pull** (`npx tsx scripts/cards-import.ts --check`, which refuses a bad file with a readable error) and reads `git log -p data/cards.csv` for exactly what changed. No GitHub Action gates the edit — edit-time validation belongs in the real admin UI (⚑26, the eventual route), so a bit of workflow funk is accepted until then. Discipline: never run `cards-export.ts` over the designer's hand-edits or his change history gets buried. — Cheap and fully diffable now; interim CI would only churn against Task 4's vocabulary migration.

## Canon sprint (2026-07-09, session 006)

46. ✅ **The per-card markdown ledger is the single source of truth for cards** (`data/cards/<color>/<slug>.md`, frontmatter + card text; `npm run cards` compiles + validates it into the engine's `generated.json`; `INDEX.md` is a generated overview). Supersedes decision 45 and session-005's single-CSV choice; `red.ts`/`yellow.ts`/`overrides.json`/`cards.csv` retire. — Card text escapes CSV quoting; one-card blast radius; per-card history; conflict-free parallel PRs; files render as card pages on GitHub.
47. ⚑ **Overextend is unit-only** — a per-attack combat gamble (decision 35) printed on units, never actions/upgrades. The 18 red actions carrying inert "Overextend N" lose it; each is re-costed or given a real cost in the canon churn. — The keyword has meant nothing on actions since decision 35; a printed word with no rules meaning is exactly the drift the canon exists to kill.
48. ⚑ **Flying is canon: "may move to any zone, ignoring adjacency."** (Was ⚑23's provisional reading; already implemented and coherent.) — Movement-freedom is a real, distinct ability; no better reading surfaced in two months.
49. ⚑ **Radiant Citadel's static is canon: "while this is in play, your opponent's Influence win threshold is +2."** Card text must say exactly that. — The ⚑23 reinterpretation works and is implemented; the fix needed was honest text, not a new mechanic.
50. ⚑ **No dead thresholds:** a card may not reference an Influence value at or beyond the win threshold. — Such text can never matter (the game is already over); it only confuses. Supreme Sentence redesigned under this rule.
51. ⚑ **Deterministic auto-targets are canon and card text must state the rule** ("...the strongest enemy unit...", ties → earliest entry) rather than implying a choice. — The engine never pauses mid-resolution (decision 24); pretending otherwise on the card is a lie a new player pays for.
52. ⚑ **"This zone" on an action means "choose a zone"** — the action declares an explicit zone target and the text says "choose a zone". — Actions have no location, so the printed phrase was meaningless (flagged on Prison Warrant, Hold the Line).
53. ⚑ **Prison stays, provisionally.** Decision 37 stands: cards normalize now, the yellow charter marks the whole package provisional, no new prison-dependent designs until the designer rules. — Removing ~15 cards on the agent's authority would pre-empt a decision that is explicitly the designer's.
54. ⚑ **Base/home rename deferred** — candidates (Banner/Hearth/Seat/Beacon) recorded in `CANON.md`'s open questions; "base" remains the canonical term until the designer picks. — Pure flavor; deferring costs nothing.

55. ⚑ *(overturned by 56, same night)* **Prison decay doubles: `prisonDecayPerUnit` 1 → 2 (rules v2.2).** Ablation sims showed prison as the structural dominator (removing imprison effects entirely: red 17% → 44%); doubling the mortgage instead keeps the mechanic and its drama while making mass imprisonment a real bet. With the balance package (this + the influence-payout restores + red curve compression) heuristic mirrors land at red ~46%, influence upsets ~3%, median 12 rounds. Prison remains "on notice" (decisions 37/53) — this is the tuned version for the designer to judge, not a verdict.

56. ⚑ **Prison decay reverts to 1 (rules v2.3)** — the post-review re-measurement. Decision 55's sims were run against a *target-blind* bot (it never cast board wipes, never valued killing jailers, could suicide its own units); the code review caught the scorer bugs, and with the fixed bot: decay 1 gives red–yellow 44%, purple–yellow 58%, and a **live influence economy** (4–10% upsets) vs decay 2's 58%/70%/~2%. — The night's best lesson: sim conclusions are bounded by policy quality; re-verify after any bot change (playtest 001, finding 2 — proven twice in one session).

## Designer session #2, evening (2026-07-10 — Griff, issue #9: the v3.0 gate closes)

59. ✅ **Hidden units can block** (Q7). Blocking isn't being attacked, so a ready Hidden unit may be assigned as a blocker — but blocking exhausts it (decision 62's default), which reveals it until it next readies, same as attacking. — The self-revealing rhythm (strike from safety, then be exposed) is the keyword's whole identity; blocking joining that rhythm keeps one rule instead of two.
60. ✅ **Sneak carries per-card payloads** (Q8): "Sneak — [effect]" is an exhaust-activated ability whose effect is printed per card (designer's examples: hit for less than full power; forbid the opponent from blocking). Builds the spec's long-deferred `activate` action; payloads reuse the existing effect-op vocabulary. — One keyword, many knives: purple's assassin identity comes from the payload variety, not a fixed number.
61. ✅ **A freed captive returns exhausted** (Q9). When the capturer leaves play, the captive re-enters its zone exhausted and readies normally at its owner's next ready step. — No same-turn revenge: killing the jailer is tempo, not tempo *plus* an instant attacker.
62. ✅ **Guard v3 = "does not exhaust to defend"** (Q10) — Guard's intercept meaning dies with the combat rework; its new perk is blocking without exhausting (block repeatedly, stay ready for its own turn). Designer: keep it simple now, buff defending Guards later only if playtesting demands. **Derived default, echoed for veto: blocking exhausts non-Guard blockers** — the perk is vacuous otherwise; this replaces the draft's "blocking is free" assumption. — A veto on the derivation flips combat's block cost and reopens Guard's design.

63. ✅ **Influence is a matchup-dependent win axis by design** (issue #6). The 4–10% mixed-matchup / 23% yellow-mirror upset spread is intended, not a bug to tune away: in the color wheel, red and yellow are the *least* influence-interactive colors; **blue and purple are to be pushed toward influence-based gameplay** as their identity. Designer is opening a dedicated color-identity thread to keep colors mapped to play styles — purple's v3 kit and any future blue design take their influence posture from that thread. — The win-rate dial for influence is color design, not the rate parameters.

64. ✅ **Claiming initiative stays** (issue #7). Designer verdict: with alternating turns, the game needs an explicit way to declare who acts first next round, and claiming is it; he's open to simpler/more intuitive alternatives if one emerges — v3 playtesting is the venue. The issue's other half (intercept feel) died with the v3 combat rework (blocker-pairing replaced the intercept window), so #7 closes. — Keep the mechanic, keep the door ajar; don't redesign what nobody has felt under the new combat yet.

65. ✅ **The five-color identity charter** (issue #10, captured in `07-COLOR-IDENTITIES.md`): RED rage/aggressive-burst (escalating pressure, momentum chains) · YELLOW honor/efficient-aggression (trades, tempo) · PURPLE wisdom/shadow reactive control (counterplay, prediction) · BLUE machines/systems control (engine building, **incremental Influence** — the influence color per decision 63) · GREEN nature/midrange anchor (board inevitability). **Mono-color decks must be playable; multi-color creativity is wanted** — the pip splash tax is the economy serving both. Blue and green are new colors, unbuilt; proposed sequencing (echoed for veto): red/yellow/purple re-churn to charter first → canon-v2.0, blue/green born after against v3 mechanics. — Every card argument now ends at this charter instead of at taste.

66. ✅ **The pip proposal is the approved baseline** (issue #9, 2026-07-11): the cost-tiered grammar (1 pip ≤2 / 2 pips 3–6 / 3 pips 7+) and its 13 deviations ship into the v3 build as-is; tuning happens after playtesting. The designer's recorded tuning lens: with mono-color pips, ask per card *"how well would this work in another color's deck — and do we hinder that with a higher pip count?"* — splash-hostility is the dial, card by card. Closes #9 entirely (Q1–Q10 + pips); the blocking-exhausts derived default (decision 62) stays vetoable until the combat build lands. — The economy layer is now designer-signed before a line of engine code, which is the loop working as designed.

67. ✅ **Upgrades orphan on death and are salvageable by either side** (v3; issue #12). When the wearer dies its upgrades stay in that zone, orphaned; as a turn action any player may attach one to a unit they control in that zone by paying the upgrade's **full cost including pips** — can't pay, can't attach. Replaces v2.3's die-with-the-wearer behavior. — Upgrades become contested battlefield objects instead of sunk costs, and the pip payment keeps salvage inside the color economy.

68. ✅ **Cost curves scale superlinearly** (issue #4): the designer explicitly rejects linear rates ("1-cost = 1 damage, 2-cost = 2 damage is what I DON'T want") — not exponential either, but 5/6/7-cost cards "should do a lot." This **supersedes the session-006 burn grammar** (face burn = cost, unit burn = cost+1 — linear by construction); Searing Bolt at 3-for-2 was its first designer override, this is its obituary. The v3 re-churn prices on a convex curve — exact rates are re-churn + playtest work, and the pip system compounds it (big cards also carry more pips, so the premium is bought with commitment, not just resources). — Card economy 101: playing a card is itself a cost, so big cards must over-deliver per resource or nobody ever banks up to them.

69. ✅ **Pips are a presence requirement, not a payment** (issue #15, revising the #9 Q1–Q2 model before it was ever built). Playing a card: exhaust any `cost` resources (color-blind), AND your resource zone must contain, per color, at least as many providing cards as the card has pips of that color. A banked card provides 1 presence of each color in its own pips — multi-color cards provide all their colors at once, same-color pips never stack (4 red pips banked = 1 red source). Pips never exhaust anything. Enables 0-cost cards with pip gates (closing the PR-#14 splash-tax escape); moves the entire splash tax into deck-building and banking choices (cf. SWU aspects). Decision 66's assignment baseline carries over as presence numbers. — Simpler at the table (one payment currency), and the color commitment lives where the designer wants creativity: deck construction.

70. ✅ **Scar's bonus caps at remaining health** (issue #12, "reading A"): bonus = min(damage marked, health − damage). A 3-health unit with 2 damage gets +1, not +2; Overextend stays cut. — The wound powers you, never past what you could survive; kills the degenerate case where a nearly-dead Scar unit hits hardest.

71. ✅ **Round 1 has no start step** (issue #20, 2026-07-11; v3 only — **reverses decision 44 for v3**). Setup banks your 2 starting resources; round 1 then opens straight into the action loop — no ready (nothing to ready), no draw, no bank. The first start step (ready → draw 2 → bank up to 1) arrives with round 2. Engine knob `firstRoundStartStep` (v2.3 keeps `true` for A/B). Sim check moved 60-game heuristic from 35–25 red to 29–31 with games ~2 rounds longer — a slower, more even opening. — The designer's read: round 1 was a phantom ritual that inflated the setup bank into 3 resources and made the opening choice feel weightless.

72. ✅ **Reckless Charge's Rush mode becomes a lunge** (issue #26, 2026-07-12 — Griff: "Option 2"). The round-scoped Rush grant was still dead on any unit from an earlier round (the free move only exists the round a unit entered) and useless on an exhausted unit (it can't move at all). 105. ✅ **A gang's retaliation is DIVIDED, not dealt in full to each — option A** (issue #84, 2026-07-15 — Griff ratified, **amends decision 84**). Griff picked **option A**: the target's strike-back is now a **single pool of its Power, poured across the unblocked attackers** — each takes up to what fells it through armor, the remainder spills to the next, and it stops when the pool runs dry (the exact mirror of the gang-**block** pour). 106. ✅ **The influence win threshold moves 15 → 20** (issue #91, 2026-07-15 — Griff: "moving the Influence bar to 20 for a win condition… 20 Life and 20 Influence… 15 comes too fast and we've made gaining influence fairly easy"). `influenceWinThreshold` 15 → 20 — the balance lever Griff chose over per-card renerfs: it slows yellow's influence clock without touching a single number printed on a card, and it reads cleanly (20 Life / 20 Influence).
