# Design Philosophy

*The north star. Everything else in this folder answers to this document.*

This game exists to be fun in a specific, testable way. Griff set down the three principles
below in [issue #99](https://github.com/booherbg/2026-card-game/issues/99) (2026-07-16), and
they are recorded here in his own words. They are not aspirations to admire — they are the
test. Every card, mechanic, rules change, and balance call is challenged against them, at
Griff's own invitation, and a design that fails one of them is wrong no matter what the sim
numbers say.

When a decision is hard, come here first. The numbers can tell us whether the game is
balanced. Only these principles can tell us whether it is worth playing.

## The three principles (Griff, #99)

### 1. Every play a different experience — but skill still matters

> Each time you play the game, it must feel like a different experience. Don't let it be
> deterministic, but don't let it be so random that skill doesn't matter. This means that you
> don't do the exact same moves or play the same cards each game. Aspects of the game will be
> similar, but the cards / actions / events have to have some type of difference. This is why
> I like deckbuilders. YOU the player get to shape a game. You have a personality and a play
> style. Here are your tools, go have fun!

### 2. The "That was AWESOME!!!" factor

> The game must illicit a "That was AWESOME!!!" factor after most, if not all, games. Games
> must ramp up to a finale and not drag on too long. Have fun. Have some imagination and
> fantasy element. Tie it to something important and worthy of that feeling.

### 3. Accessible to everyone

> The game must be accessible to a variety of people. This means that you don't have to spend
> hours agonizing over it just to be able to play a game. You don't have to spend tons of
> money to be competitive (which is often the case in modern TCGs). It also must be inclusive
> to a wide audience of men/women, U.S/foreign, etc.

## How this gets used

When reviewing a design — a card, a mechanic, a balance change — the agent asks Griff's own
questions of it:

- **Does it keep games different?** (principle 1 — variety with skill)
- **Does it serve the finale, the "AWESOME"?** (principle 2 — the ramp, and not dragging)
- **Does it stay accessible?** (principle 3 — no hours to learn, no money to win, wide welcome)

A design that trades one of these away for a better sim number is a bad trade. These come first.
