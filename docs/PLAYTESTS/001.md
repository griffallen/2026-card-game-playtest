# Playtest 001 — canon-v1.0 balance pass (simulation campaign)

**Date:** 2026-07-09 (session 006)
**Rules version:** v2.2 (canon-v1.0) — `prisonDecayPerUnit` 1 → 2 shipped this pass (decision 55)
**Decks:** Crimson Assault (red, post-churn) vs Radiant Order (yellow, post-churn)
**Method:** headless sims — heuristic and random policies, alternating seats, seeds 1000+, N=100–300
per configuration. The heuristic bot was upgraded mid-pass (see finding 2); numbers before/after the
upgrade are not comparable.

## Findings

1. **The card churn alone broke red** (18% in heuristic mirrors, down from ~36% pre-churn). The
   yellow re-costs — especially the cheaper utility actions — outweighed red's gains, and red's
   deck-doubles rule had silently swapped its two doubled workhorse bodies for utility actions
   (fixed: doubles are the cheap core minus Warpath/Pillage, plus Rageforged Brute + Volcanic Slam).

2. **The bot was lying to us.** The greedy heuristic scored targeted plays without looking at the
   target — an imprison on a 1/1 scored the same as on the 8/8, burn was effectively
   random-targeted, and killing a jailer (which frees every captive) carried no value. After making
   it target-aware, red jumped 18% → 38% *with zero card changes*. **Moral: sim conclusions are
   bounded by policy quality; re-verify balance claims after any bot change.**

3. **Prison is the structural dominator** (ablation: removing all imprison effects → red 44%,
   influence wins 14%). Not the bombs, not the recurring jailers — the core recurring lockup loop.
   Doubling the decay (the "mortgage") was the clean, theme-positive fix: mass imprisonment is now
   a real bet against your own win axis. Red 38% → ~46% with decay 2 alone.

4. **Guard's free interception — the handoff's prime suspect — is nearly irrelevant** to the
   matchup (ablating the keyword moved red ~1 point). The "too sticky" worry can rest.

5. **Sample noise is real at N=300:** the shipped package measured 45.7% red on one deck-ordering
   and 55.3% on another (a cost change reorders the generated deck list, which changes every
   shuffle). Honest claim: **red ~45–55%, even within bot noise.**

## Open questions this feeds

- Influence wins at 1–3% under *competent* play: the track is a random-play punisher more than a
  real second axis. If Griff wants it to matter vs good players, the levers are bigger event
  payouts or a lower threshold (his open question #7 on the demo Audit page).
- Red under random play (30%) vs heuristic (~50%): red rewards piloting — likely intended
  ("volatility"), worth confirming at the table.
- Prison's fate (decision 37) — v2.2's mortgage is the tuned version to judge, not a verdict.
