## Summary

The day the table learned to explain itself. It opened with two housekeeping asks — the
Chronicle restructured into voiced-intro + technical-log entries (the format this file's
journal twin now follows; a short unlogged session just before this one had built the
Chronicle page itself), and the 4-minute watch loop re-armed — and then Blaine sat down and
playtested for hours, filing a dozen issues, and nearly every one turned out to be the UI
or the engine failing to say what it knew.

**The explain-yourself arc, in order.** His #57 loss to a yellow influence win exposed the
recap filter dropping influence lines (the guard-payout economy was deciding games
invisibly); the follow-up audit checked every engine log line against the filter and found
shield soaks, disarms, pumps, heals, forced exhausts, volleys, and orphan drops all
silently excluded. Griff's #58 "blocking is broken" report turned out to be correct engine
math betrayed by wording — twin "takes 5 damage from the blockers" lines read as one
combined pool, so counter hits now name their source, the block window lists every pairing
live, and a tap cycles a blocker between attackers (the defender draws the pairs, which
the spec always granted and the UI never exposed). #62 got the block window its math
(per-attacker power, breakthrough tags, a live let-through line with lethal flags and the
retaliation reminder); #64's "how is it healing?!" caught start-of-round triggers logging
anonymously — automatic ticks now sign their card ("heals 2 — Censer of Purity"); #61 made
gray orphans name their one failing gate; #63 gave table units hover glosses per keyword.
The audit rule earned its keep twice more: decision 100 was live in the engine but taught
nowhere (spec, rulebook, help panel, and Guard gloss all still claimed lone attackers
unblockable, full stop — Blaine's own game log disproved them at t11), and session 009 had
left the typecheck gate red (duel-law test) and the server tests asserting stale pool
counts.

**The Sellsword's Primer (#59).** A versioned player guide in the voice of Old Varga, a
retired sellsword who has made every mistake the Primer warns against at least twice —
three acts of a game, banking doctrine, duel-law combat, and a chapter per color with
playset advice. Blaine's edit passes were a lesson in themselves: the planted-error conceit
dropped as adversarial, "losing to the calendar" and friends caught in a deep language pass
that also surfaced two factual bugs (the banking math said 6-by-round-4; round 1 has no
bank step, it's round five — and Radiant Citadel's +2 threshold was sold as three rounds of
tolls). He also banned "load-bearing" and the other AI-isms outright (saved to memory).

**Measurements over arguments.** #60's deck-limit question got an experiment, not an
opinion: Griff's Red 65 vs its own dense 48, 800 games, identical seeds — 51.3% vs 49.3%,
a wash; curation dominates size. Griff then supplied the structural reasons (no dual
resource economy, no rarity) and the whole exchange is decision 101. Later his #66
Breakthrough redesign became decision 102, the siege clause — in the opponent's Home a
Breakthrough attacker leaves no damage behind (blockers → target → base) — TDD'd, folded
into all five teaching surfaces, and priced honestly: zero movement in 800 bot games,
because bots never siege units in Homes; it's a human-play weapon.

**The bot stands accused (#68).** Blaine's rush-deck games (#67 — won as rush, then won
again as burn when the bot blocked plan A) produced the evidence: the Machine declined
free blocks with a ready 2/7 wall in one game and took the identical block in the next,
and banked its Guard at 8 life under siege. Filed as a tracked defect — erratic policy,
not missing capability — with candidate fixes and the standing caveat that any ai.ts
change invalidates every benchmark and must ship with a full re-run. In support, the
chronicle became bot-tuning telemetry: bank lines note the hand kept (both seats), block
windows note who could have blocked vs who was sent, passes note the options passed up
(vs-AI and watch modes only; hotseat stays hand-hidden).

**Quality-of-table, shipped same-hour off live feedback:** amber banking mode with its own
Skip button; Enter/Esc/P keyboard driving (opening bank included) with the keys marked on
their buttons (#65, Griff); the selection glow recolored from invisible parchment to cyan

## Decisions

- 101 ✅ Deck size stays min-48/no-max — measured (800 games, a wash) + Griff's structural
  reasons (unified resource system, no rarity); the 4-copy cap is the real constraint.
- 102 ✅ The siege clause: Breakthrough in the opponent's Home spills through the unit
  target into the base. ⚑ agent readings: plain damage absorbs first; shields spill nothing.
- Chronicle format: voiced intro + technical log per entry (wrap ritual updated in CLAUDE.md).
- Engine log doctrine, twice applied: damage names its source (#58), automatic ticks sign
  their card (#64). The recap filter is audited against the engine's full log vocabulary.
- The chronicle is telemetry: decision context (banks/blocks/passes) rides in vs-AI logs.
- #68 filed: bot defense is erratic, not absent — fix must land with full benchmark re-runs.
- Blaine: no "load-bearing"/AI-isms in writing (memory); Varga speaks in game objects, not
  planted errors.
