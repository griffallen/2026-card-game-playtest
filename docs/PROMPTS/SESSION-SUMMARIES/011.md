## Summary

The night began with a 4-minute watch loop and a standing instruction: sweep, triage, and **delegate up to Fable** for mechanics-shaped work. The first ripe ticket was #68 (the bot won't defend). A Fable subagent found the root cause — `blockScore` prices a *dying* blocker at full sticker value, so a wall at 2-of-7 health looks "too precious" to spend on a free, winning block; the block-vs-decline margin collapses inside the rng jitter and the bot coin-flips it away — and banked a one-line fix. It was held, not shipped, because it moves balance numbers and #55 was mid-ratify.

Then Griff woke up and **machine-gunned a yellow rebalance**: twelve card PRs (#69–73, #75–80) and a demo-UI issue (#74). The through-line was clear and coherent — *shrink the bodies, enrich the payoffs*: count-scaled influence (per-attacker Vanguard, per-exhausted Prison), condition-relaxed auras (Hierophant at "positive" not 10), defensive keywords traded for raw stats (1/1 Shielded Sunguard, 2/2 Armor-1 Bulwark), a modal Containment Priest (capture **or** exhaust), a self-cloning Radiant Citadel, and Shackles (a reusable debuff-upgrade). Every PR got the three-question review; the mechanics-shaped wiring went to a standing Fable agent kept alive across ticks by SendMessage.

Two engine capabilities were built to serve the pass. **Decision 103**: a count-scaled `per` modifier on the influence and heal ops (`{count:'attackers'}` and `{count:'units', f}`), TDD, unlocking #70 and #71. **Decision 104** (Griff's ruling): *no caps on life or influence* — they're just numbers effects move; only the win/loss thresholds end the game (heal overheals past 20, life can read negative, influence is unclamped). Both landed on branch `feat/per-attacker-influence` with #70–73 wired.

The benchmark then told a hard truth and set off the most important thread of the night. The wired batch **over-corrected** — red 56–70% against the stock yellow deck, well past Decision 99's 46% parity target, the statline nerfs being the every-game lever. Griff pushed back: the sim can't grade a defensive color when the bot *won't defend*. He was right. Re-run under V3 (the live duel law), the over-correction held — but the caveat stood, because both pilots were the same defense-blind bot. Griff chose the honest path (a): **make the bot defend, then re-measure.**

So the banked #68 fix grew into three: the block-decline pricing fix, a bank-selection tax so it stops banking Guards under siege, and bounded defensive valuation so it *deploys* its walls and captures. TDD, 209 tests, **landed to main** on Blaine's "let's go for it." The re-benchmark was the night's cleanest result: a competent defender pulls current-stat yellow from red 52% to **red 46% — dead parity** (vindicating Griff), yet the batch's body-nerfs *still* push red to 53–60% even with the good bot. Both truths at once.

Griff reframed again, and better: hold the stat sweep entirely — the *stock deck's curve* is the real problem — wire the cards, let him build a **proper yellow deck**, and benchmark that. He proved it live in #81: his yellow deck **beat red**, winning on Influence at 15 while a single Fortress Keeper (Guard) walled a Bloodfrenzy'd Juggernaut off the base round after round and the clock never stopped. He tempered the read himself — the guard wall is *strong* against a bot that can't crack it; nerfs are still coming — which reconciled cleanly with the parity number: a wall crushes a weak pilot, a good one contests it.

Alongside the balance work, the demo grew. The **block-assignment pop-up (#58)** was built — a spatial modal where you drag or click defenders under attackers, gang them with pour-order badges, or wave one through with a live life-loss preview, plus a "view board" peek toggle Griff requested — verified end-to-end and merged to main. The **card-type icons (#74)** were rendered at true badge size and iterated with Griff to a locked set (🧍 unit, 🏃 action, ↑ upgrade) after the flat pawn vanished in night mode and the emoji arrow dragged a blue box that fought the color-coding.

The night's one genuine wall: **the demo cannot be deployed from this session.** `scripts/deploy-demo.sh` gates on a Playwright playability check that needs localhost, and this environment network-isolates localhost per-process (server binds, nothing else can reach it — confirmed for the main shell *and* a subagent, sandboxed and not).
